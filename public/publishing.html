What is music publishing?

Every song earns two kinds of money: the recording, and the song itself — the melody and lyrics. Publishing royalties are what the song itself earns: streams, radio, TV, live performance, YouTube, social video, print.

When your song is played, someone owes you. But nobody pays you automatically. The song has to be registered with the right societies, and the money has to be collected from each one. That registration and collection is what publishing administration is.

✦ ☽ ✦

Why your PRO alone isn't enough

Your PRO — ASCAP, BMI, or another — collects performance royalties, and that's only part of what a song earns. Mechanical royalties, international collections, and money from YouTube and social video are collected elsewhere, and only if your songs are registered there.

Most independent artists have publishing income sitting uncollected because no one registered the songs everywhere they needed to be. Administration closes that gap: registration everywhere it counts, and someone chasing what you're owed.

Questions, answered

What does publishing administration cost?

Nothing upfront. 15% of the publishing royalties we collect for you. If nothing is collected, nothing is owed.

Do I keep my copyright?

Yes — 100%. This is administration, not a publishing deal. No rights are transferred, no co-publishing.

Do I keep my PRO?

Yes. Your own ASCAP, BMI, or other PRO affiliation stays yours.

What do you actually do?

We register your songs and writers with the societies that pay, and collect the publishing royalties they earn — performance, mechanicals, streaming, YouTube and social video.

How long until I see royalties?

Publishing moves slowly. Societies report and pay on their own schedules, so it takes months from registration to money in hand. That's normal — and it's why the sooner songs are registered, the better.

What songs can you administer?

Your own original songs, where you own or control the publishing. If a song has co-writers, the splits need to be agreed between the writers first.

Do you take a cut of my streaming or master income?

No. The 15% is on collected publishing royalties only. Your recording and master income is untouched.

How long is the term?

One year. Before it ends, we'll agree together whether to continue — nothing rolls over without you saying so.
